Why Coachella Valley Pavement Fails Early — And How We Build Around It

Standard asphalt mixes and sealcoat schedules are built for milder climates. In the Coachella Valley, triple-digit summer heat softens asphalt and accelerates oxidation, intense UV exposure breaks down binder faster than almost anywhere else in the state, and sudden monsoon downpours test drainage and base work that was never designed for flash runoff. We spec materials, timing, and base prep around those three things on every job — not a one-size-fits-all approach built for a cooler coastal market.

Heat & Oxidation

Sun-exposed lots and driveways oxidize and crack faster here. We schedule sealcoating on a tighter cycle in high-exposure areas instead of a generic annual calendar.

UV Degradation

UV breaks down asphalt binder over time, which is why striping and surface sealant fade faster in open desert lots than shaded or coastal ones — we use materials rated for it.

Monsoon Runoff

Flash storms expose bad grading fast. We check drainage and base compaction before we resurface anything, so a new surface doesn't fail at the first summer storm.
